The six checks — 5 of 6

Forty years of good work. Three reviews.

Buyers read reviews before they read your website. A firm with decades of good work and a handful of old reviews doesn't look experienced — it looks unproven, next to a younger competitor with a wall of recent ones.

Get a free visibility audit See the system

What we check

how many reviews you have, how recent, and what they average; how that compares to the firms you're actually competing with; whether reviews sit on the profiles buyers check or are scattered where nobody sees them; whether anyone responds — an answered review tells a different story than a silent profile.

What a finding looks like

a contractor operating since 1962 with three Google reviews; a testimonials page that still shows placeholder text where the praise was supposed to go; a firm claiming 900+ five-star reviews on its site while its public profiles display none that a buyer can verify.

What good looks like

a steady, recent body of reviews on the profiles that matter, growing as the work completes — because asking became part of how the work closes.

Examples are based on findings observed across construction and design firms. Details may be combined or simplified to protect the businesses involved.
